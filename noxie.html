UX case study 05  /  Game UX  /  NOXIE: PRIME CUT

Three playtests.
Three new first minutes.

A six-minute action-comedy game with a horror section, made in four days for a game jam. Every scripted test passed. I still got lost each time I played it. This is how three playtests, written down word for word, rewrote the way the game teaches itself.

ProjectEntry for Epic AI MegaJam 2026, team Vyztra
My roleGame director and playtester
TimelineNov 12 to 15, 2026
StatusSubmitted Nov 15, 3 hours 21 minutes before the deadline. Results due around Jan 29, 2027.
01The challenge

The tests passed. I was lost.

You play Noxie, a small blue creature sent on an errand for a "lightbulb" that turns out to be a living glowgrub. Street, rooftops, arcade, a soft-play hall, a breaker room where dolls move when you look away, and a prize-crane boss.

The verbs are simple: shoot, gulp and bash. Hold the right mouse button to gulp a prop or a small guard, then fire it back as a named special shot. Simple to describe. Hard to learn in the first minute, with no one beside you to explain it.

  • 01

    No goal on screen

    You started in the street with nothing telling you where to go or why.

  • 02

    Damage before the lesson

    You could die before you knew the gulp move existed.

  • 03

    Hidden state

    Nothing clearly showed whether a gulp had loaded a special shot.

02Three playtests

My words, then the fixes.

AI agents built and tested the game under my direction. I played it, said what I saw out loud, and those words were written down as they were said. Each playtest became that day's fix list.

Playtest 1  /  Nov 13, 13:40
"Mouse sensitivity is insanely high."

Also: a captured enemy "is not visually readable", and "The sound effects feel cheap."

  • Mouse speed lowered, with [ and ] keys to adjust it
  • Hints on dark plates, never smaller than 26 px
  • A belly window that shows what you've captured
  • The character redesigned
Playtest 2  /  Nov 14, build 5b
"There's no goal or mission. You're just thrown into it and not knowing what to do."
"I didn't know I had to go find the gulper to begin with, so I died pretty much immediately."
"Maybe when the game starts, it, like, pauses and you have to, like, click or something to acknowledge"
"I feel like there should be probably the main function that acts as just like a regular weapon"
"The break room is thoroughly confusing because I don't know what keeps killing me."
  • A premise card at the start, and no damage until the Gulper card is dismissed
  • An objective line and a marker; a player who does nothing survives 90 seconds
  • My weapon idea adopted: the basic shot never runs dry, and a gulp loads a named special
  • Breaker room: an entry card, a look-away lesson, a 0.9 second red-glow wind-up, a red HIT arrow, and progress that survives death
  • A windowed mode
Playtest 3  /  Nov 15, 10:45
"I like the foundation."
"I could not clearly tell whether gulping an object actually changed my special ammunition."
  • A magenta LOADED: ... SHOT plate and ring
  • Doors that slam at the door line
  • An explainer card for the prize hall, and a marker on the open power cell
  • A GULP THIS marker
  • A 1.1 second camera hold on the first Cut
03The rule

"Do not downgrade these observations because automated tests passed."

My instruction to the team with the third playtest, Nov 15. The final packaged chapter passed 509 of 509 scripted checks. None of them could measure whether a first-time player knew what to do.

The insight

Scripted tests passed while I was lost, three times. Next time: a first-minute spec before the first build, and one cold player a day.

04The solution

Teach it on screen, one thing at a time.

05Results

What I can prove, and what I can't yet.

3

Playtests, each one turned into that day's fix list.

509

Scripted checks passed on the packaged chapter, out of 509.

60

Frames per second, measured 59.5 to 60, with 17 of 17 clean exits.

3:21

Hours and minutes to spare at submission.

About 18 packaged builds were made; I played 3 of them. The jam brief asked for 15 to 25 minutes of play; the game shipped at about 6. Play it at justleonard.itch.io/noxie-prime-cut, or read the dev log.

Not proven yet: a cold player. No one outside the project has playtested it, and nobody playtested the final submitted build. There are no accessibility options: threats are cued by red alone, with no control remapping or subtitles, and one flash frame goes over the project's own limit. A controller is untested and the sound was never checked by ear. Jam results are due around Jan 29, 2027. This slot stays empty until those are answered.

My role

  • Game direction
  • Playtesting, recorded verbatim
  • First-minute and onboarding calls
  • Directing a team of AI agents

Tools

  • Unreal Engine 5 on an Apple silicon Mac
  • AI agents for building and scripted testing
  • Built with an AI-assisted workflow

What I'd tell the next team

  • Write the first-minute spec before the first build.
  • Put one cold player in front of it every day.
  • Never cue a threat with colour alone.
