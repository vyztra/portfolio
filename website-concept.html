Case study 02  /  Dealer website concept  /  AgRevolution

Putting the truck
on the homepage.

A farm-equipment dealership built its business on bringing the shop to the farm. Its website didn't show it. I audited the site, studied the market, and rebuilt the experience around the service truck, on the company's real data.

ClientAgRevolution. Self-initiated concept, not commissioned.
My roleStrategy, UX, visual and motion design, prototyping
Timeline7 days in Oct 2026, then prototype v4 in Nov
StatusWorking prototype. Not live.
01The challenge

The best part of the business was three taps deep.

AgRevolution is an AGCO-owned dealership group selling and servicing Fendt, Massey Ferguson and other brands across Kentucky, Indiana, Illinois and Ohio. Its edge is on-farm service: trucks based near the farms, and a manager who calls before anyone is dispatched.

Its website runs on the manufacturer's shared dealer template. It's clean and well kept. It could also belong to any other dealer on it.

  • 01

    It asks which store, not where the machine is

    On a phone, the service form takes 3 taps and a scroll to reach, and opens with a store list. There is no fast path for a machine that's down.

  • 02

    No phone number on the phone

    The number sits inside a closed menu. The person with a machine down is standing in a field, holding a phone.

  • 03

    No store pages

    Every location record sends you back to the homepage, so nothing can rank for "tractor repair near Henderson".

  • 04

    Slow to show a tractor

    Inventory pages were slow to show their main photo on a phone, and the grid jumped as it loaded.

02AI as research staff

AI as research staff and a hostile review panel. Not a pixel machine.

Measure first, then design. AI did the legwork at a scale one designer can't: crawling, testing and cataloguing. I set the questions, read the evidence and made the calls.

499

URLs crawled from the live site's sitemap.

16

Speed tests on the key page templates, plus an accessibility scan and tap counts for each farmer task.

23

Dealer groups studied, plus manufacturer sites and brands from outside farming.

353

Fendt and Massey Ferguson dealer websites checked for the platform behind them. That's how I found the shared template.

The insight

None of the 23 dealer groups shows a farmer which truck covers their farm. AgRevolution already had that data on its own map. It just wasn’t showing it.

03The hostile review

I built it, so I didn't grade it.

My first concept was easier to use than the live site and slower on a phone, because I'd sent a full-size photo to every phone. Running the same tests on both caught it. A demo never would have.

So the rebuild went to three independent AI reviewers: a hands-on tester, a strategy reviewer, and a simulated panel of farmers, a service manager and AgRevolution leadership. They found a defect in each of the three main customer journeys within the first minute. I re-verified each one, fixed them the same afternoon, then built the panel's top request: "I'm at the machine", one tap to drop your location on an aerial map.

Measured with the same tests used on the live site. The prototype ran on a local server without the tracking scripts a live site carries, so these are best-case numbers. The 94 was measured on the October build. Re-run with the same phone test on Nov 4, 2026, prototype v4 scored 86, and 100 after its review fixes (v4.1).

Not proven yet: business results. The rebuild isn't live and hasn't been tested with farmers, so there are no request, call or sales numbers. This slot stays empty until there are.

What I'd tell the next team

  • Test your own work the way you tested theirs.
  • Design for the cab: a phone, rural signal, a machine down.
  • Let the company's own data carry the story.
